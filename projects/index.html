The order was ready. The landing cost wasn't.

01 / Brief

A real buyer, a real order, a checkout that stalled.

I ran a live procurement need through Alibaba.com end to end — and through a second B2B platform for comparison — to see exactly where an Indonesian buyer slows down.

The need was concrete: 100 pairs — 200 individual headlamp units — of Toyota Avanza 2013 front headlamp assemblies, delivered to Palembang, with exact fitment, a formal quotation, and a protected payment route. I used the Alibaba app with Indonesia as the delivery market and English as the interface.

I searched, shortlisted only Buy Now listings, compared supplier evidence, built the checkout, reviewed every local payment method, and created a wire-transfer order without paying it. The listing sold in 50-set lots, so I tested the checkout at that minimum before committing to the full trial. The same task ran on Made-in-China; Amazon's international checkout served as a reference for how landed cost can be shown.

The order reached IDR 295.3M. Shipping alone was IDR 121.2M — 69.6% of the item subtotal — and the checkout did not say whether Indonesian duties and taxes were included. I stopped before payment. That pause is the finding.

The buyer I represented

  • Procurement decision-maker for a small automotive business.
  • 100-pair trial; the listing sells in 50-set lots.
  • Needs fitment, side and pair quantity, OEM reference, and a written quote.
  • Will pay by a protected method — after the total is confirmed.
  • Won't pay without a landed-cost breakdown.

Privacy: no personal data is published. Addresses, phone numbers, and payment fields are blurred or cropped; supplier identity was removed where it was not part of the finding.

02 / Method

Four stages, scored like a buyer would.

A task simulation plus heuristic scoring, so every friction point has evidence, a score, and a severity — not just an opinion.

01

Define a real requirement

Product, quantity, destination, certifications, and deal-breakers — written before opening the app.

02

Search on both platforms

Same keywords on Alibaba and Made-in-China, then OEM part numbers when the broad query failed.

03

Evaluate Buy Now listings only

Only add-to-cart products qualified, so the evaluation tested an orderable path, not an inquiry.

04

Build the checkout

Shipping, currency, every local payment method, processing fees, and order protection.

05

Create an unpaid order

Wire transfer selected, order submitted, then the unpaid record inspected in My Orders.

06

Annotate and score

Every friction point captured, scored 1–5, and ranked by whether it could stop payment.

04 / Findings

What actually stops the order.

Six friction points, ranked by what they do to a high-value purchase. Five can stop payment; the sixth is a strength that needs better disclosure.

Finding 01

High

Fitment is a range, not a guarantee.

Titles say “Avanza 2012–2015” or “2012–2017”, but not 2013-specific compatibility, left or right side, connector, or mounting points. The buyer has to open chat before trusting the listing.

Finding 02

High

“Set” is never defined.

Price tiers are quoted per set. A set could be one lamp or a pair, so the unit that every landed-cost comparison depends on stays ambiguous until the supplier answers.

Finding 03

High

Freight is the real price.

IDR 121.2M of freight sits on an IDR 174.1M item. At 69.6% of the subtotal, shipping changes the decision more than the unit price does.

Finding 04

High

Duties and taxes are a blank line.

DAP is shown, but the checkout does not say whether Indonesian import duty and tax are included or charged on arrival. A buyer cannot approve an incomplete total.

Finding 05

High

“Create Order” is not “Pay”.

Selecting wire transfer submits the order to the supplier; it then sits in My Orders as unpaid. The button and the next step do not make that distinction clear.

Finding 06

Medium

Local payment is a strength — if limits are visible.

Five payment routes, IDR pricing, and visible order protection are real advantages. But the 2.99% fee and GoPay's IDR 20M cap appear only inside the method panel, after the buyer has committed to checkout.

05 / Fixes

Six changes that would keep the order.

Each fix maps to a finding and can be tested inside the existing flow. None of them requires a new marketplace — only clearer disclosure.

01

Price the unit completely

Show per lamp, per pair, and per side at the card, the tier, and the checkout — then repeat it in the order summary.

02

Show the landed-cost equation

Item + freight + estimated duty and tax + processing fee = payable now. Flag anything estimated and anything payable on arrival.

03

Give freight choices

Air courier for samples, LCL for mid-size orders, FCL for bulk — each with transit time, delivery scope, insurance, and customs support.

04

Rename the button and show the path

“Submit order for confirmation”, followed by a six-step bar from order to shipment. Make the unpaid state impossible to miss.

05

Make trial quantities visible

Show sample and trial tiers next to the 50- and 100-set prices, so a first order can fit the buyer's actual demand.

06

Carry the reference into payment

On the provider page: order number, merchant, amount, deadline, limits, and the currency conversion — before the buyer confirms.

What already works

  • Prices shown in IDR with an exchange-rate reference.
  • Local payments: bank transfer, cards, GoPay, DANA, Kredivo.
  • Order protection, guaranteed delivery, and a refund policy.
  • Supplier rating, response time, and on-time dispatch visible.
  • A direct checkout instead of an inquiry-only flow.

What I would not add

  • 01 Another dashboard before the total is complete.
  • 02 A fee that appears only after the payment method is chosen.
  • 03 A “Buy Now” badge on a listing whose fitment is still unconfirmed.

Reference pattern

This is not a competitor comparison. It is a pattern reference for how a cross-border checkout can show options, fees, and the payable total before the buyer commits.

The buyer did not abandon the product. The buyer abandoned an incomplete total.

01 / Brief

A quote is not a cost until it is normalized.

Four 1688 quotations for 200 catnip ball toys arrived in four different formats. Two looked close on unit price. Delivered, they were not.

The request was specific: 200 catnip ball cat toys, retail-ready, shipped in cartons no larger than 60×60×60 cm and no heavier than 22 kg. Four suppliers responded with a unit price, a freight estimate, and a per-unit add-on for barcode labelling and packing.

Comparing the headline unit prices would have produced the wrong award. Freight ranged from ¥11.00 to ¥26.00 per unit, and one supplier's carton weight made the shipment non-compliant before price mattered at all.

So every quote was rebuilt into one number — delivered cost per unit — and screened against the constraints the order actually had to meet. The result was an award a manager could sign without opening another spreadsheet.

What had to be on one basis

  • Product, size, and retail packaging
  • Quantity: 200 units
  • Unit price in RMB
  • Freight cost per unit
  • Barcode and labor add-on
  • Carton dimensions and gross weight
  • Certifications and supplier history

02 / Method

Six steps from four quotes to one number.

The method is repeatable. The same logic can be applied to the next RFQ without rebuilding it from scratch.

01

Fix the specification

Product, size, packaging, barcode requirement, and quantity — written down before any supplier was asked to quote.

02

Request quotes on identical terms

Same product and same 200 units, so the only variables left were price, freight, and service.

03

Normalize to delivered cost

Unit price plus freight per unit plus the barcode and labor add-on — one comparable number per bid.

04

Apply the carton constraint

Checked dimensions and gross weight against the 60×60×60 cm and 22 kg limits before comparing totals.

05

Verify the supplier

Years on the platform, certifications, response speed, and whether a second listing told the same story.

06

Write the award rationale

One page naming the winner, the excluded bid, the two open risks, and the conditions to fix in the PO.

The awarded supplier

  • Lowest delivered cost at ¥19.70 per unit.
  • FDA, BSCI, EN71, and CE listed on the supplier profile.

  • Fastest reply of the group.

The bid I excluded

  • 01 Its ¥9.50 unit price was only 12% above Supplier C.
  • 02 At ¥26.00 freight per unit, delivered cost reached ¥35.60 — 81% above the award.
  • 03 Its 32.4 kg carton exceeded the 22 kg limit, so the order could not ship as quoted.

04 / Risks

Two open items before the PO.

An award is not an order. These are the two things that had to be closed in writing before money moved.

Risk 01

Carton data is a claim, not a measurement.

Supplier B's 32.4 kg carton shows how quickly a packing detail changes the freight. Supplier C's carton dimensions and gross weight had to be confirmed in writing before booking — not taken from the listing.

Risk 02

Certificates were listed, not supplied.

FDA, BSCI, EN71, and CE appeared on the profile. The award required the actual files, covering the exact product model, attached to the purchase order as a condition of the order.

05 / Decision

Award Supplier C. Fix the add-ons in the PO.

Supplier C won on delivered cost and evidence. The award memo fixed the details suppliers most often change after the quote.

01

Dimensions and gross weight signed off before the forwarder is booked, so the freight number is based on a measurement.

02

Attach the certificates to the PO

FDA, BSCI, EN71, and CE files, naming the exact model they cover — not a screenshot of a badge.

03

Fix barcode and labor at ¥0.20

Written into the PO as a fixed per-unit cost, so the delivered total cannot move after approval.

Why A lost at +5.1%

Supplier A matched the winning unit price of ¥8.50. It lost on freight: ¥1.10 more per unit, or ¥200 across the order, with no extra evidence attached. The backup position is worth keeping — the award is not.

What I would not do

  • 01 Compare ¥8.50 with ¥9.50 and call it a 12% gap — delivered, it was 81%.
  • 02 Approve a quote without a measured carton.
  • 03 Treat a certificate list as a certificate file.
The award went to the bid that survived the constraints, not the one that looked cheapest in the chat.
