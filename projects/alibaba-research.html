01 / Brief

A cheap listing is not a safe part.

Aftermarket LED headlamp bulbs for a Toyota Avanza/Xenia looked cheap and comparable on the surface. The cost of the wrong fit was not: safety, warranty disputes, and returns.

Alibaba offered hundreds of near-identical listings, price tiers that moved with quantity, and a checkout total that was not clear enough for internal approval. The brief was to evaluate Alibaba as a sourcing channel for a 100-set trial, compare it with another B2B platform, and produce a recommendation a manager could approve without asking for more context.

The work had to answer three questions in the right order: will it fit the exact vehicle, what does it really cost delivered, and can the supplier evidence quality and warranty in writing? A recommendation built on the headline unit price would have failed all three.

Because the supplier conversation and the internal approval used different languages, the evaluation was written in English and Bahasa Indonesia from the start.

The decision I had to make

  • Will it fit this vehicle — not just “Avanza” in the title?
  • What is the true delivered cost, including shipping and duties?
  • Can the supplier prove quality, warranty, and lead time in writing?
  • Which payment method protects the trial order?
  • Can a manager approve this without another meeting?

02 / Method

Seven stages, one evidence trail.

I built the search around the vehicle and part number, then narrowed by what could actually be verified. Each stage left a screenshot or a written check behind it.

01

Search by vehicle and part number

Started with the exact Toyota Avanza/Xenia application and OEM references, not a generic “LED headlamp” keyword.

02

Filter for exact fit

Checked OEM cross-reference, socket type, voltage, and heat-sink design before comparing price.

03

Verify the supplier

Reviewed supplier type, years on the platform, audit report, and response history.

04

Compare commercial terms

MOQ, price tiers, lead time, and what was actually included at each tier.

05

Calculate delivered cost

Item, shipping, and duties — not the headline unit price that appears in search results.

06

Audit payment protection

Confirmed the protected payment route and what it covers before recommending an order.

07

Document the evidence

Captured annotated screenshots at every stage and wrote the recommendation bilingually.

04 / Decision

Sample first. Then a controlled trial.

The recommendation was not to buy the cheapest listing. It was to buy evidence first, then test the supplier with an order small enough to contain the risk.

Recommended route

Sample first.

Then a 100-set trial using a protected payment method.

01

Order samples and verify the physical fit

Check the connector, housing fit, finish, and delivered specification against the listing before any production order.

02

Get the evidence in writing

Request test reports, warranty terms, and the exact model covered by the supplier's claims.

03

Control the trial

Place a 100-set trial with a protected payment method, and confirm duties and delivery terms before checkout.

Why not just pick the cheapest?

The lowest unit price did not include the delivered cost. The recommendation had to survive questions about fit, warranty, and payment protection — and a lower headline price would not have answered any of them.

What I would not do

  • 01 Order 1,000 sets on a unit price alone.
  • 02 Treat a listing's wattage claim as test evidence.
  • 03 Approve without confirming duties and warranty in writing.
The goal was not the cheapest listing. It was the listing we could defend.
